Erdős problem #251

The prime-gap dyadic series

Is the dyadic series of consecutive primes irrational? Equivalently, is the corresponding consecutive-prime-gap dyadic series irrational?

The problem

The question

List the primes as 𝑝1 =2,𝑝2 =3,𝑝3 =5,…p1=2,p2=3,p3=5,…. Divide them by 2,4,8,…2,4,8,… and add: 𝑃 =∑𝑛≥1𝑝𝑛/2𝑛P=∑n≥1pn/2n. Erdős asks whether this exact infinite total can be a fraction. The denominators grow fast enough for the sum to converge; its rationality is open.

An example

The first five terms give 2/2 +3/4 +5/8 +7/16 +11/32 =101/322/2+3/4+5/8+7/16+11/32=101/32. Later terms increase the sum to about 3.67464393.6746439. The gap from 3 to 5 is 5 −3 =25−3=2; that same extra 2 appears in every prime from 5 onwards.

Is ∑𝑝𝑛2𝑛∑pn2n irrational? (Here 𝑝𝑛pn is the 𝑛nth prime.)

Erdős problem #251, as erdosproblems.com/251 states it

The setup in detail

Write the primes in increasing order as 𝑝1 =2,𝑝2 =3,𝑝3 =5,…p1=2,p2=3,p3=5,…. The series is 𝑃 =∑𝑛≥1𝑝𝑛2−𝑛P=∑n≥1pn2−n: each next denominator is twice the previous one. The question is whether the limit of these running totals is rational. Every finite running total is a fraction, but the limit of rational numbers can be irrational.

The long record proves the elementary bound 𝑝𝑛 ≤1250𝑛4pn≤1250n4. This is enough for convergence: the ratio of consecutive bounds is

1250(𝑛+1)4/2𝑛+11250𝑛4/2𝑛=12(1+1/𝑛)4<34(𝑛≥10).1250(n+1)4/2n+11250n4/2n=12(1+1/n)4<34(n≥10).

The remaining terms are therefore bounded by a convergent geometric series. Finite changes to the primes alter the total by a rational number, so they cannot change its rationality. The difficulty lies in controlling all later terms, rather than computing a longer initial segment.

The example in detail

Putting the first five terms over a common denominator gives

22+34+58+716+1132=32+24+20+14+1132=10132=3.15625.22+34+58+716+1132=32+24+20+14+1132=10132=3.15625.

The next terms are 13/64,17/128,19/256,…13/64,17/128,19/256,…; the full total is about 3.67464393.6746439. This gives its size, without deciding rationality.

Define the gaps by 𝑔𝑛 =𝑝𝑛+1 −𝑝𝑛gn=pn+1−pn. Their first values are 1,2,2,4,2,…1,2,2,4,2,…, and each prime is 2 plus its preceding gaps: 11 =2 +1 +2 +2 +411=2+1+2+2+4. In the drawing this is the height of the column for 11, split into the floor and four horizontal bands.

The highlighted gap from 3 to 5 contributes 2 to every prime from 5 onwards. Its contribution to the sum is therefore

28+216+232+⋯=2(18+116+⋯)=24.28+216+232+⋯=2(18+116+⋯)=24.

The successive widths halve, and their running total approaches one quarter. This explains the red band’s width and its area, rather than introducing a new quantity alongside the prime sum.

The crux

Column 𝑛n has height 𝑝𝑛pn and width 2−𝑛2−n, so its area is one term. Read the same staircase horizontally: a floor of height 2, then one band per prime gap. The red band has height 2 and width 1/8 +1/16 +⋯ =1/41/8+1/16+⋯=1/4, hence area 2/42/4.

Writing 𝑔𝑛 =𝑝𝑛+1 −𝑝𝑛gn=pn+1−pn, this gives 𝑃 =2 +∑𝑛≥1𝑔𝑛/2𝑛P=2+∑n≥1gn/2n. Either both sums are rational or neither is. The theorem below constructs altered gaps with rational sums while retaining many gap properties. Their running totals need not be primes: the original question remains open.

One area, two sums. The column over the first half of the ruler has height 2, the next quarter has height 3, and so on. The sum of their areas is the prime series. Read horizontally instead: the floor contributes 2(1/2 +1/4 +⋯) =22(1/2+1/4+⋯)=2. The band for gap 𝑔𝑛gn has width 2−𝑛−1 +2−𝑛−2 +⋯ =2−𝑛2−n−1+2−n−2+⋯=2−n. Consequently

𝑃=2+∑𝑛≥1𝑔𝑛2−𝑛.P=2+∑n≥1gn2−n.

All areas are nonnegative, so this regrouping preserves the total. Adding the rational number 2 does not change rationality. It is therefore enough to study the gap sum, but it is still the same open question.

What the theorem tests. Could broad properties of the gaps alone force irrationality? Add six units to two neighbouring gaps. The four splits give different weighted changes:

08+616=616,28+416=816,48+216=1016,68+016=1216.08+616=616,28+416=816,48+216=1016,68+016=1216.

Every later running total rises by six whichever split is chosen. The choice therefore changes the weighted sum without changing the later remainders modulo 2, 3 or 6. The next diagram repeats this operation at increasingly separated indices and with larger divisibility requirements.

The proved statement. Start with any convergent series 𝐴 =∑𝑛≥0𝑎𝑛2−𝑛−1A=∑n≥0an2−n−1 of nonnegative integers, any initial cutoff 𝐾K, and any bound 𝑓(𝑛) → +∞f(n)→+∞. One fixed set of allowed indices after the cutoff supports corrections attaining every target in a nondegenerate interval above A. The corrections are nonnegative integers and satisfy 𝑒𝑛 ≤𝑓(𝑛)en≤f(n) eventually. The allowed indices occupy a proportion tending to zero uniformly over long intervals. For each fixed positive integer q, beyond a cutoff depending on q, every correction and every cumulative correction is divisible by q. The allowed set, target interval and cutoffs are chosen before the target.

The prime boundary. Applied to prime gaps, with eventual bound log⁡(𝑛 +3)log⁡(n+3), this gives altered gaps with rational sums, the same eventual residues for each fixed modulus, and the same limiting distributions of fixed-length consecutive gap blocks. Their running totals still grow like 𝑛log⁡𝑛nlog⁡n. These totals need not be prime: for instance, adding six replaces 19 by 25. The construction shows that the retained properties are insufficient; it proves no rationality or irrationality assertion for the actual prime series.

Results and open questions

#251. A public Lean declaration constructs a synthetic sequence of positive even digits bounded by 4log⁡(𝑛 +1) +244log⁡(n+1)+24. Its dyadic sum is exactly 66, every complete scaled tail and every tail shift is integral, the values 22 and 44 recur arbitrarily late in every residue class, and its increasing odd cumulative positions satisfy 𝑃𝑛/(𝑛log⁡𝑛) →1Pn/(nlog⁡n)→1. The reasoning paper states the combined theorem explicitly. This rules out a much richer package of plausible soft inputs than unboundedness or nonperiodicity alone, but these digits are not the actual prime gaps. The declaration is source-backed and does not yet have a curated claim row.

Independently, a sparse perturbation of the prime gaps can have a rational dyadic sum while retaining the prime growth scale, every fixed eventual congruence, and asymptotically the same short-block statistics. The later positions are not asserted to be prime. This is complementary to Land’s conditional result, not a refutation. Lean checks the sparse rational-target construction and the polylogarithmic schedule with growing-block transfer in SparsePaperR11.lean. The printed construction additionally gives congruence cutoffs uniform in the target; the linked Lean statement quantifies those cutoffs after the target.

The prime-growth corollary uses cited analytic results. This obstruction shows that those coarse statistics alone do not force irrationality. Lean also checks a precise conditional route: for one fixed shift, cofinally many adjacent tail shifts strictly between −1−1 and 11, with unequal actual prime gaps, would prevent eventual integrality of that shift. Those prime-specific mismatches are not proved. The exact prime-gap summation-by-parts equivalence still proves neither the prime-gap series nor the original series irrational. Statements with Lean proofs

Lean is software that verifies a formal proof against its theorem statement and assumptions. These proofs establish only the statements listed here.

  • The finite summation-by-parts identity relating the prime dyadic partial sums to the prime-gap dyadic partial sums, with an exact endpoint term and no convergence premise.
  • The factor-of-two normalisation between the two displayed indexings, at every finite horizon.
  • The unconditional prime-to-gap identity, using an elementary polynomial bound on primes to prove summability; irrationality of the prime series is equivalent to irrationality of the prime-gap series.
  • For an abstract integer-digit dyadic tail recurrence: the exact block identity, and that a shift of fixed length is integral exactly when an explicit scaled state is.
  • That one integral shift propagates to every later index.
  • That repeated doubling removes the power-of-two denominator, so every rational-valued dyadic recurrence has one fixed positive shift integral at all sufficiently late indices.
  • For a real-valued dyadic recurrence, cofinal non-integrality of every fixed positive shift implies irrationality of the initial state.
  • That the actual consecutive-prime-gap sequence is unbounded, by the factorial prime-free-interval construction, and therefore is not eventually periodic with any positive period.
  • For any integer-digit dyadic tail recurrence, two adjacent fixed-length shifts lying strictly between -1 and 1 cannot both be integral when the corresponding digits differ.
  • For the actual prime gaps, a cofinal supply of adjacent small-shift pairs with a mismatching gap excludes eventual integrality of that fixed shift.
  • The generic distance-to-integers core of the finite-truncation criterion: an approximation farther than its error bound from every integer certifies non-integrality.
What these proofs do not establish
  • The target irrationality.
  • A cofinal nonintegrality theorem for the concrete prime-gap tails; defining the series and proving its recurrence do not supply this.
  • A theorem supplying cofinally many adjacent actual-prime-gap tail shifts in the open unit interval with the required gap mismatch.
  • Any theorem forcing periodicity or automaticity of the prime gaps from rationality; the free-carry identity disconfirms that route.

ErdosProblems.Erdos251.irrational_tsum_primeDyadicTerm_iff_primeGap
abstract Lean-checked classification and normal form; novelty unassessed family: dyadic_tail_integrality_classification

For any integer-digit real dyadic tail recurrence T(N + 1) = 2*T(N) - g(N + 1), the initial value T(0) is irrational exactly when every positive-length tail shift is nonintegral.

This exact normal form covers arbitrary integer-digit dyadic tail recurrences with the declared real state type; it does not supply a cofinal nonintegral shift for the consecutive-prime-gap recurrence. The prime-specific producer remains open, so no prime-gap irrationality, novelty, significance, or external-review claim is made and Erdős #251 remains open.

Assumptions still needing proofs

Prove any one of these and the corresponding reduction above becomes unconditional.

  • prime_gap_cofinal_shift_escape For every fixed ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every N0, find N ≥ N0 for which ∑𝑗≥1(𝑔𝑁+ℎ+𝑗 −𝑔𝑁+𝑗)/2𝑗∑j≥1(gN+h+j−gN+j)/2j is not an integer.
  • cofinal_adjacent_small_mismatch For every fixed ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every N0, find N ≥ N0 such that both adjacent full tail shifts have absolute value less than 1 and 𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1 ≠𝑔𝑁+1gN+h+1≠gN+1. The checked local consumer then excludes eventual integrality of the h-shift.
