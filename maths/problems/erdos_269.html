Erdős problem #269

Three-prime running least common multiples

For a finite set of at least two primes, is the sum of reciprocals of the running least common multiples of the smooth numbers irrational? This library treats the three-prime case.

The problem

The question

Choose a finite set of at least two primes. List 1 and every positive integer made from those primes, in increasing order. After each number, find the least common multiple (LCM): the smallest positive integer divisible by everything listed so far. Add its reciprocal, even when this running value repeats. Must the infinite sum be irrational, meaning it cannot be written as a fraction of two integers?

An example

Allow 2, 3 and 5. The list starts 1,2,3,4,5,6,…1,2,3,4,5,6,…; the running multiples are 1,2,6,12,60,60,…1,2,6,12,60,60,…. Adding 5 changes 12 to 60. Adding 6 leaves 60 unchanged, since 6 already divides 60. Both numbers contribute: the first six terms give 1 +1/2 +1/6 +1/12 +2/60 =107/601+1/2+1/6+1/12+2/60=107/60.

Let 𝑃P be a finite set of primes with |𝑃| ≥2|P|≥2 and let {𝑎1 <𝑎2 <⋯} ={𝑛 ∈ℕ :if 𝑝 ∣𝑛 then 𝑝 ∈𝑃}{a1<a2<⋯}={n∈N:if p∣n then p∈P}. Is the sum ∑∞𝑛=11[𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛],∑n=1∞1[a1,…,an], where [𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛][a1,…,an] is the lowest common multiple of 𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛a1,…,an, irrational?

The setup in detail

Read the statement symbol by symbol. 𝑃P is a finite set of primes, at least two of them, such as {2,3}{2,3} or {2,3,5}{2,3,5}. The set in braces holds every positive whole number 𝑛n whose prime divisors all belong to 𝑃P. It is infinite, since it contains every power of each prime in 𝑃P, and 𝑎1 <𝑎2 <𝑎3 <⋯a1<a2<a3<⋯ lists it in increasing order.

The bracket is the least common multiple: the smallest positive whole number that each of 𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛a1,…,an divides. For example [4,6] =12[4,6]=12. It is a running least common multiple, recomputed each time one more number joins, so the sum has one term for every 𝑛n, and equal terms are all counted.

Irrational means it cannot be written as 𝐴/𝐷A/D with integers 𝐴,𝐷A,D and 𝐷 >0D>0. A proof must rule out every denominator 𝐷D at once, and no amount of computed decimal places can do that.

The question is asked for every finite set of at least two primes. With one prime the sum is rational: for 𝑃 ={2}P={2} the list is 1,2,4,8,…1,2,4,8,…, the running least common multiple of the first 𝑛n numbers is 2𝑛−12n−1, and

1+12+14+18+⋯=2.1+12+14+18+⋯=2.

For an infinite set of primes, Erdős (1988) called irrationality a simple exercise. Two primes are settled, as the crux explains; three or more primes are open.

The example in detail

The next numbers are 8,9,10,128,9,10,12. Including 8 =238=23 requires a third factor 2, changing 60 to 60 ×2 =12060×2=120. Including 9 =329=32 changes 120 to 360; 10 and 12 already divide 360. Thus the first ten terms, grouped over the common denominator 360, are:

The two differ by 8/360 =1/458/360=1/45: the second copy of 1/601/60 and the extra two copies of 1/3601/360.

The sum is finite. From 2𝑎2a to just below 2𝑎+12a+1 the list holds at most (𝑎 +1)2(a+1)2 numbers: the exponents of 3 and 5 are each at most 𝑎a, giving (𝑎 +1)2(a+1)2 choices, and each choice leaves room for at most one power of 2, since doubling leaves the range. Each of their terms is at most 1/2𝑎1/2a, because 2𝑎2a is already in the list. These bounds add up to ∑𝑎≥0(𝑎 +1)2/2𝑎 =12∑a≥0(a+1)2/2a=12. The crux

The diagram continues this example to 30. The staircase shows the running multiple; each rise multiplies it by the labelled prime. Red flat stretches mark repeated values. The bottom row groups their contributions: four numbers sharing 720 add 4/7204/720. Counting each value once loses these repeats and changes the question. Steve Fan settled the two-prime question. For three primes, the result below rules out a finite factorisation extending his method; the original irrationality question stays open.

Why the staircase rises only at prime powers. Up to 24 the largest allowed prime powers are 16,9,516,9,5, whose product is 16 ×9 ×5 =72016×9×5=720. Every allowed number up to 24 divides this product: for example, 24 =8 ×324=8×3. Conversely, the running LCM must contain each of those three powers. Generally it is the product of the largest available power of each prime. A new prime power adds one factor of its prime; other numbers leave it unchanged. The staircase's horizontal spacing counts listed numbers, rather than measuring their numerical distance.

A different sum that is settled. Suppose we count each distinct running value once. After the value L, multiply the remaining sum, its tail, by L. Each subsequent value is at least twice the preceding one, so this scaled tail lies between 0 and 1. If the complete sum were A/D, D times each scaled tail would be an integer: all earlier denominators divide L. Only finitely many scaled tails could then occur.

For primes 2, 3 and 5, the short paper proves that a scaled tail determines both the next block of prime-power jumps and the next tail. Finitely many states would therefore force eventual periodicity, just as finitely many remainders force repeating digits in long division. If each repeating block spanned ℓℓ doublings and contained 𝑐c powers of 3, their long-run frequencies would require 2ℓ =3𝑐2ℓ=3c, impossible. This proves irrationality for the distinct-value sum. Erdős asserted the conclusion for every finite set of at least two primes in a 1973 letter without printing a proof; the paper claims no priority for this conclusion.

Why repeats matter. For the original sum, multiply the contribution from 16 to just below 32 by the preceding running value 360:

4⋅360720+3603600+2⋅36010800=136.4⋅360720+3603600+2⋅36010800=136.

The scaled tails are no longer below 1. The long paper proves that those taken at powers of 2 grow quadratically with the exponent. Under rationality, D times these tails would still be integers, but unbounded integers need not repeat. The distinct-value proof therefore does not settle the original sum.

The factorisation route. Steve Fan proved transcendence, and hence irrationality, for every pair of distinct primes by factoring the repeated sum into two series and applying a theorem of Loxton and van der Poorten. The result below rules out extending the table factorisation to three distinct primes by any finite sum of separated products. It does not rule out a different proof. The original question remains open for finite sets of three or more primes.

Results and open questions

#269. For three pairwise distinct primes, the threshold-column argument produces nonsingular selected kernel minors of every order and excludes every finite rational separated kernel representation. The arbitrary-order theorem is present in the compiled public Lean source, but the Wave-A receipt’s named axiom audit does not audit that rank declaration; Comparator checks only the displayed rank-two minor −1/15−1/15. For the actual {2,3,5}{2,3,5} series, Lean checks the rationality-to-positive-reduced-carry bridge. Cofinal local-window escape then extinguishes such carries; under the explicit cap hypotheses recorded in the claim registry, that escape statement is equivalent to irrationality. This is an exact interface, not an easier theorem whose premise has been proved. The source-specific escape and three-prime irrationality remain open.

For every pair of distinct primes, both the repeated running-LCM reciprocal sum and the version retaining one term at each distinct LCM value are transcendental. The ordinary proof expresses them as nonconstant quadratic and affine polynomials in one transcendental Hecke–Mahler value. Steve Fan posted the repeated-sum two-prime factorisation, reduction and transcendence conclusion first, on the problem’s forum on 26 June 2026; the note credits his priority, gives a proof found independently, derives the de-duplicated formula, and uses the cited Hecke–Mahler transcendence theorem. Lean also checks the two-prime affine and quadratic formulas and derives transcendence from the cited Hecke–Mahler result as an explicit named hypothesis; that external theorem itself is not formalised here. Statements with Lean proofs

Lean is software that verifies a formal proof against its theorem statement and assumptions. These proofs establish only the statements listed here.

  • That for three pairwise distinct primes the least common multiple of the smooth prefix equals the product of the three maximal pure prime powers below the cutoff.
  • That equal logarithmic coordinates give an equal prefix least common multiple, so the running value is constant on logarithmic cells.
  • That advancing exactly one logarithmic coordinate multiplies the running value by the corresponding prime.
  • That the positive power channels of distinct primes are disjoint, so the first powers in three channels give an exact jump count.
  • That a finite smooth lattice sum groups exactly by running height, with the height fibre cardinality as coefficient.
  • A quadratic bound on the cardinality of the actual filtered exponent shell.
  • That each dyadic block contains at most one internal pure power from each non-dyadic channel, so the compressed {2,3,5} block radix belongs to the exact alphabet {2,6,10,30} and in particular lies between 2 and 30.
  • That the smallest {2,3,5} kernel rectangle has determinant exactly −1/15−1/15, so its two-by-two matrix has rank two over the rationals and no rank-one separation exists.
  • The canonical least-positive-residue interval and congruence facts, including the zero-residue representative.
  • The exact local-window identity for an integral carry.
  • For the actual {2,3,5} series, rationality produces a positive reduced carry; cofinal local-window escape rules out such carries under the recorded bound.
  • A bounded-radix dichotomy: under a bounded positive digit alphabet, a nonzero scaled tail must be separated from the integers cofinally.
  • Exact three-channel block-nullity as a coboundary condition, and extinction of a bounded carry lift under two independent anchors.
  • The exact residue-digit plus coboundary form of an integral carry, together with a function-faithful observer criterion and a finite realised-span consequence.
  • Irrationality of the distinct-height sum for {2,3,5}, which counts each running value once.
What these proofs do not establish
  • Irrationality of the repeated sum, or transcendence, in any three-prime case.
  • CofinalLocalWindowEscape for those actual words; it is an explicit hypothesis of the checked consumer, not a theorem.
  • A proof that the actual three-channel word satisfies the block-null, anchor, or factorisation hypotheses of the migrated abstract consumers.
  • The infinite limit of the jump expansion; only its finite core is checked.
  • Any unbounded denominator exclusion. The finite certificates exclude denominators below an explicit bound and no further.
