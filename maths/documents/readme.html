Plectis

Plectis is research on eight problems posed by Paul Erdős: a short paper and a longer record for each problem, Lean proofs of selected statements, experiments and failed approaches. On the public maths map, every result in the sixteen problem papers appears with its Lean statements and Comparator records.

Open the maths map · Inspect one result · Formal Conjectures · Papers · Repository map

Use it

  • In a browser, with nothing to install: open the maths map, select Expand map and search for a theorem or a Lean declaration. Selecting a result shows its statement, its Lean statements and its Comparator record.
  • As a reader, without Lean: start with A reader’s way in. The glossary explains project terms.
  • In a terminal, with Git and Python 3.11 or later: clone this repository and run python3 scripts/verify_claims.py --claim finite_prime_weighted_support. This prints one claim’s statement, Lean declarations, Comparator interface, paper and limits. It finds the declarations in your checkout and does not run Lean. REPRODUCIBILITY gives the clone command and the steps for rebuilding a proof in Lean.

Inspect one result

Problem 257 asks whether the series

∑a∈A12a−1\sum_{a\in A} \frac{1}{2^a-1}

is irrational for every infinite set AA of positive integers. That question remains open. Theorem 1.2 of the #257 short paper proves irrationality at every integer base at least two for infinite sets that meet a finite-prime weighted condition. The records for this theorem are:

  • Statement: Theorem 1.2 on the map.
  • Lean proof: divisibilityWeightedClaim. The evidence record sets the printed theorem beside the Lean statements.
  • What Lean establishes: that the exact formal statement in that file is proved.
  • What Comparator states: run 35935225572 passed, comparing the proof with the same statement written separately from Mathlib, with only the axioms propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice allowed.
  • What neither tool establishes: whether the formal statement matches the intended mathematics, and novelty, priority or significance.
  • Who has reviewed it: Bo Cowgill approved commit 40f7826a of Formal Conjectures request #6528 on 10 October 2026. Later commits followed, and the request is open with review required. Palomar registered a Lean selection containing this theorem. The paper has not been peer reviewed.

Status and the use of AI

The one formal answer among the eight problems concerns #1041. Using the degree-seven polynomial constructed by the erdosproblems.com contributor ani, Lean proves that every preconnected strict-lemniscate set containing two distinct roots has one-dimensional Hausdorff measure greater than two. This refutes the exact Formal Conjectures path-image-length statement; the separate total-variation bound is also checked. The other seven targets remain open. Independent human review of correspondence with the 1958 wording has not been recorded. Comparator checks only selected exact statements, axioms and kernel acceptance; it does not assess novelty or historical correspondence.

ani’s original counterexample is credited in the #1041 case study. Results and limits gives each problem’s statements with their assumptions, earlier work and remaining questions.

Will Cook directs this independent, AI-assisted prototype and is not a mathematician. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. He built and directed the infrastructure, reviewed claims when he could and has not independently verified every claim. He maintains the sources and corrects the public record. Novelty and significance need human judgement. The records include calculations and routes that stopped, so that other people can question, repair or reuse them. The system paper describes how results, corrections and credit are recorded.

Formal Conjectures contributions

Formal Conjectures is Google DeepMind’s open repository of conjectures stated in Lean. Five contributions from this project have merged into it. The crosswalk lists each request with its reviews and compares the eight problems with the Formal Conjectures statements.

  • #6505 answers the exact #1041 formal statement in the negative, credited to ani, and corrects a hidden hypothesis in the file. The case study follows it from statement to proof.
  • #6529 adds Erdős’s classical result for infinite sets with summable reciprocals, at integer bases at least two. Arbitrary infinite sets remain open.
  • #5034, #6506 and #6507 record Lean proofs of solved variants of #258, #257 and #1049. All three rest on one theorem, Erdős’s result of 1948.

A merge records that a maintainer accepted that exact change and covers no other claim here. Fourteen more requests are open with review required. An individual approval or comment applies to that request at the commit the crosswalk names. These reviews cover selected contributions, and no review covers the whole corpus.

Corpus inventory and counting limits

Corpus at a glance

The reviewed layer a mathematician should judge: 160 curated claim records in 38 contribution families, reaching Lean source through 478 principal declaration links. docs/SCOPE.md gives its shape and docs/RESULTS.md gives the strongest checked result per problem. The website and the papers are the human reading path.

The rest is engineering inventory. About 87% of the 164,639 declarations (142,668 across 695 modules) are machine-emitted certificate shards: one integer checked prime, one position excluded. The remainder is not all hand-written either.

Generated shards are counted as formal source and never as separate mathematical claims. Claim records cover every status, including cited and open, and are partitioned once. These are navigation counts, not novelty claims. They still need expert validation.

What Lean and Comparator verify

Lean checks whether a proof establishes the exact formal statement in its source. The source map connects paper passages to those declarations, claim records list their status and limits, and prior art identifies earlier results.

Comparator checks a solution against a separately declared formal statement, with an explicit list of permitted axioms. formalization.yaml lists the selected statements. The verification dossier records selected statements across all eight problems. Researchers judge correspondence with the intended mathematics, novelty and usefulness, and methodology explains the review required to change a claim.

Contribute

Corrections, references, counterexamples and clearer explanations need no Lean. Use the research-progress form, email Will or work on a paper. CONTRIBUTING covers the rest, and accepted contributions receive credit under the credit policy.

Read or verify locally

REPRODUCIBILITY starts with Git and Python 3.11+, then explains how to install Lean and check a proof. Its claim check inspects source links without compiling the proof. For research with a coding agent, see AGENTS.md and the agent workbench.

The paper links each headline result to the relevant source. For a particular topic, start with the source map; it gives the module order without asking you to decode Lean declaration names first.

Videos

There are videos of the maths map, Erdős #257 and the Plectis system. The videos also show private interfaces. Do not infer results from private or unreleased work.

Other records

Palomar checks Lean proofs and publishes registered results with their review comments. The submission record separates verification, editorial review and registration. The Palomar guide explains the local preparation checks; passing those checks alone does not establish acceptance.

plectis-erdos-lean holds the Comparator and Palomar packages, and the Prove2Me records cover #243 and #257. The paper catalogue links versioned aiXiv editions, which may differ from the current papers.

Credit

One mathematician’s comments on the #243 note led to all sixteen problem papers being rewritten for a first-time reader. The credit ledger lists each piece of advice and what it changed. Names stay withheld until each person confirms they want to be named.

Citation and licence

Use CITATION.cff and record the commit used. For mathematics, cite the relevant problem paper and the original sources it uses: prior art and source attributions separate earlier results, formalisation and local use.
