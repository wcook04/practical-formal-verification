Erdős problem #1041

Short connections inside polynomial lemniscates

Must two roots of a monic polynomial in the open unit disc be joined by a sub-two-length curve inside its unit lemniscate? Ani’s degree-seven example refutes the exact Formal Conjectures statement; correspondence with the 1958 wording awaits human review.

The problem

The question

Put 𝑛 ≥2n≥2 roots strictly inside the circle of radius 1 about 0, and form 𝑓(𝑧) =(𝑧 −𝑧1)⋯(𝑧 −𝑧𝑛)f(z)=(z−z1)⋯(z−zn). This is monic: its leading coefficient is 1. At a point 𝑧z, |𝑓(𝑧)||f(z)| is the product of its distances to the roots. Call a point allowed when that product is less than 1.

Any two roots are less than 2 apart in a straight line. The question is whether some pair can always be joined in under 2 while staying entirely on allowed ground.

An example

For 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑧2 −0.81f(z)=z2−0.81, the roots are −0.9−0.9 and 0.90.9. Between them, |𝑓(𝑥)| =0.81 −𝑥2 ≤0.81 <1|f(x)|=0.81−x2≤0.81<1. Their whole straight segment is therefore allowed, and its length is 1.8. This polynomial answers yes; the question asks whether every permitted polynomial has such a path.

Let 𝑓(𝑧) =∏𝑛𝑖=1(𝑧 −𝑧𝑖) ∈ℂ[𝑧]f(z)=∏i=1n(z−zi)∈C[z] with |𝑧𝑖| <1|zi|<1 for all 𝑖i. Must there always exist a path of length less than 22 in {𝑧 :|𝑓(𝑧)| <1}{z:|f(z)|<1} which connects two of the roots of 𝑓f?

Erdős problem #1041, as erdosproblems.com/1041 states it

The setup in detail

A complex number 𝑧 =𝑥 +𝑖𝑦z=x+iy is a point (𝑥,𝑦)(x,y) in the plane. Its modulus |𝑧| =√𝑥2+𝑦2|z|=x2+y2 is its distance from 0; |𝑧 −𝑤||z−w| is the distance from 𝑧z to 𝑤w. Multiplication multiplies moduli, so

|𝑓(𝑧)|=|𝑧−𝑧1|⋯|𝑧−𝑧𝑛|.|f(z)|=|z−z1|⋯|z−zn|.

The allowed region is therefore specified entirely by the roots: multiply the distances to all of them and ask whether the answer is below 1. Every root is allowed, since one factor is zero. The leading coefficient must be 1; multiplying the polynomial by a large constant would change the region.

A path is a continuous curve, and its length counts the whole distance travelled. The straight distance between any two roots satisfies |𝑧𝑖 −𝑧𝑗| ≤|𝑧𝑖| +|𝑧𝑗| <2|zi−zj|≤|zi|+|zj|<2. The issue is containment: a short straight segment may cross forbidden ground. The question asks for at least one suitable pair, for every permitted polynomial. Two occurrences of a repeated root give a constant path of length zero, so a counterexample must have distinct roots.

ani’s fixed degree-seven polynomial answers this precise question no. The paper proves the stronger statement that every connected subset of its allowed region containing two roots has one-dimensional Hausdorff measure greater than 2. This measure counts length of a set without choosing a route through it; a path’s travelled length is at least the measure of its image.

Erdős, Herzog and Piranian posed the historical question in 1958, and Pendyala proved the degree-four case. ani posted this counterexample in September 2026. The supplied Lean sources verify the stated total-variation and Hausdorff-measure formulations. Independent human review of their correspondence with the 1958 wording is not recorded. The proof fixes one polynomial; ani’s wider small-parameter family is not proved here.

The example in detail

For 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑧2 −0.81f(z)=z2−0.81, a path between the real roots −0.9−0.9 and 0.90.9 must cross the imaginary axis: its horizontal coordinate changes continuously from negative to positive. On that axis write the crossing point as 𝑖𝑦iy. Then

𝑓(𝑖𝑦)=−𝑦2−0.81,|𝑓(𝑖𝑦)|=𝑦2+0.81.f(iy)=−y2−0.81,|f(iy)|=y2+0.81.

Thus the only allowed crossings have 𝑦2 <0.19y2<0.19, or |𝑦| <0.436…|y|<0.436…. This vertical interval is a passage that every allowed root-to-root path must cross.

Reaching a crossing 𝑖𝑦iy from either root costs at least the straight distance √0.81+𝑦20.81+y2. Hence the whole path has length at least 2√0.81+𝑦220.81+y2. At 𝑦 =0y=0 this is 2 ×0.9 =1.82×0.9=1.8, and the horizontal segment achieves that length while staying allowed.

Moving the roots closer to the unit circle makes the passage narrower, but the horizontal segment still works. A narrow passage alone is therefore insufficient for a counterexample. The seven-root construction also puts its passage where reaching it from the two roots already costs more than 2.

The crux

The hatched petals are allowed ground in ani’s seven-root counterexample; blank ground is forbidden. Only the red roots A and B lie in the same connected piece. Their dashed straight segment is 1.95 long but leaves the hatching.

The right-hand circle magnifies the centre. The red passage P lies slightly above 0: every allowed A-to-B path must pass extremely close to P. That tiny detour makes every such path longer than 2. The result below explains the length bound.

The drawing uses two scales because the roots are about one unit from 0 while the passage is only about a trillionth of a unit away. Hatching always means allowed points, and red always marks the two arms containing A and B.

  1. Start with seven petals. The polynomial 𝑧7 −1z7−1 has seven roots evenly spaced on the unit circle. Its allowed region has seven petals whose boundaries touch at 0; 0 itself is forbidden. ani changes its coefficients slightly and moves every root to distance 𝜌 =1 −10−96ρ=1−10−96 from 0, strictly inside the circle. These changes are invisible in the left view but alter how the petals meet.
  2. Find the only pair that can be joined. The paper verifies that five connected pieces contain one root each and the sixth contains A and B. Paths cannot jump between pieces, so only this pair needs a length estimate.
  3. Locate every possible crossing. On the two-root piece, each ordinary polynomial value has two preimages. Cutting the disc of values along a suitable segment separates these into a side containing A and a side containing B. Any joining path must cross the preimage of the cut. A local quadratic estimate places that whole passage within 𝜌𝜀/3750ρε/3750 of P, where 𝜀 =10−12ε=10−12. Enclosing the whole passage rules out an alternative crossing farther away.
  4. Pay for reaching the passage. The paper’s root enclosures give |𝐴 −𝑃| +|𝐵 −𝑃| >𝜌(2 +0.143𝜀)|A−P|+|B−P|>ρ(2+0.143ε). Moving the crossing away from P can reduce each distance by at most the passage’s enclosing radius. Therefore every allowed joining path has length greater than 𝜌(2 +(0.143 −2/3750)𝜀) >2ρ(2+(0.143−2/3750)ε)>2. The excess is tiny but strictly positive.

The dashed route through 0 would cost |𝐴| +|𝐵| =2𝜌 <2|A|+|B|=2ρ<2, but part of it crosses blank ground. The close-up shows why moving from 0 to the compulsory passage matters. Results and open questions

#1041. A source-backed ordinary theorem covers monic degree-𝑛n polynomials, 𝑛 ≥3n≥3, whose distinct zeros lie on a circle of radius 𝜌ρ and satisfy 2𝜌𝑛 ≤12ρn≤1: two adjacent zeros have chord length at most 2𝜌sin⁡(𝜋/𝑛) <22ρsin⁡(π/n)<2, and their straight chord lies in {|𝑓| <1}{|f|<1}. A repeated zero is an immediate short connection. The reasoning record explains the alternation and chord argument. The proof is outside Lean; an exact finite checker tests load-bearing identities and configurations, and the theorem does not yet have a curated claim row. At each fixed degree, the sharp arc constant 22 leaves radii sufficiently close to 11 outside this method.

Every monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc also has radial root-to-origin segments inside {|𝑓| <1}{|f|<1}, so any two roots join through the origin with length less than 22; Lean checks the complete displayed trinomial statement. For collinear roots, Lean checks a sharp Chebyshev segment bound and the resulting <2<2 connector when the roots lie in the open unit disc. These are solved families, not a general connector theorem. For a squarefree monic polynomial, write 𝜇 =min𝑓′(𝑐)=0|𝑓(𝑐)|μ=minf′(c)=0|f(c)|. An ordinary theorem gives a connector of length less than 22 in the open unit lemniscate whenever 𝜇 ≤13/25μ≤13/25, with no root-location hypothesis; scaling gives a connector of length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n in {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The sharp critical-value mean on the closed unit disc is Lean-checked with source-bound audit evidence, and the paper also gives an ordinary proof. The mean controls critical values, not connectors.

A further ordinary theorem gives a connector of length less than 22 in every degree 𝑛 ≥3n≥3 when a simple nonzero critical value admits the stated separated critical-value disk at radius 4/34/3; it asserts no existence of such a disk. On the generic stratum where simple nonzero critical values have pairwise distinct arguments and moduli, an ordinary slit-sheet theorem identifies the inverse-ray root-connection tree but gives no uniform length bound. Lean checks Newton-flow decay, ray-separating translations, and perturbative root retention. Using one degree-seven polynomial constructed by the erdosproblems.com contributor ani, Lean proves that every preconnected strict-lemniscate set joining two distinct roots has one-dimensional Hausdorff measure greater than two.

This refutes the exact Formal Conjectures path-image-length statement and the separate total-variation formulation; correspondence with the 1958 wording remains unreviewed. The short paper also gives a cubic showing why a two-root first merger alone does not force a capacity gap; its Lean capacity clause assumes the classical transfinite-diameter formula. Statements with Lean proofs

Lean is software that verifies a formal proof against its theorem statement and assumptions. These proofs establish only the statements listed here.

  • For ani’s degree-seven polynomial, every preconnected strict-lemniscate set containing two distinct roots has one-dimensional Hausdorff measure above two. Lean refutes the exact Formal Conjectures path-image claim and checks a separate total-variation bound.
  • Along a trajectory tangent to the complex Newton vector field -f/f', the polynomial value satisfies w' = −𝑤=−w.
  • The exponentially rescaled polynomial value is a first integral, so the value of a Newton trajectory remains on one oriented ray.
  • A finite Newton-flow connection can only join endpoints whose polynomial values lie on the same positive ray; distinct critical-value arguments therefore exclude such a connection.
  • For two translated critical values, the ray-collision locus is a real affine line with an exact one-parameter description.
  • A finite family of ray-collision lines can be avoided by an arbitrarily small common translation.
  • An explicit root-retention estimate keeps roots of a sufficiently small constant perturbation inside the unit disc.
What these proofs do not establish
  • The full small-parameter family reported by ani; only one explicit polynomial is formalised.
  • Independent human review adjudicating whether the exact Formal Conjectures path-image-length statement represents the 1958 curve-length wording.
  • The spanning-tree decomposition claimed in the March 2026 manuscript; its printed Proposition 12 proof uses an invalid three-ended local saddle block, and a Cassini example refutes the printed global tree budget.
  • A repair of Proposition 12 that retains the printed 1/(2𝜋)1/(2π) spanning-tree coefficient; any later argument must change the metric inequality.
  • A perturbation theorem that first makes critical values injective while preserving the relevant lemniscate component, collars, and strict length slack.
  • A corrected restricted-family or alternative-metric gluing theorem; universal sub-two total-variation gluing is ruled out by the degree-seven counterexample.

locally proved result; novelty unassessed family: translation_avoidance

An arbitrarily small common translation separates a finite injective family from zero and from shared positive rays.

Finite-family avoidance does not provide the global topological or metric gluing.
