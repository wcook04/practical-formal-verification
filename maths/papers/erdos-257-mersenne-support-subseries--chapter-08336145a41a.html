Short paper

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Summary. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper covers the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It does not establish the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Sources and formal verification

Erdős’s reciprocal-summable extension and his fractional-part argument are on pp. 222 and 226 of [7]. For the periodic theorem of Luca and Tachiya [12] we used their own account [11], and for Hornich’s strict-tail theorem we used Nitecki’s proof [10]; we have not consulted the two original articles. The Formal Conjectures file [6] gives a Lean statement of Problem 1.1, and no proof here depends on it. We make no claim of priority for the comparison of the support classes.

Theorem 1.2 is proved in Lean 4 with Mathlib as the declarations divisibilityWeightedClaim and finitePrimeWeighted_fixedBase_hereditary, and the mixed theorem has a Lean proof as well; a separate Lean construction of a separating support 𝑉V uses finite sets of squarefree divisors. Among the auxiliary results, Lean proofs are also available for the finite estimate (S), the scalar bound behind (7), and the tail comparison, injectivity and measure dichotomy for achievement sets. The formal proofs establish the stated theorems, and the printed arguments are ordinary proofs written for the reader. The calculation for 𝐴⋆A⋆, the construction with fresh primes, Corollary 5.2, the signed extension of Theorem B.1 and Theorem D.1 are proved here by ordinary arguments, and Corollary 5.2 has no formal counterpart. The remaining supporting declarations are listed in the final source section of the companion paper, grouped by support criteria, finite denominators, integer recurrences and achievement sets.
